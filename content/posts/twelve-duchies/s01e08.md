A couple of hours later, rain falls steadily over the ruined temple, steam rising from the scorched timbers. Outside the walls, three narrow graves lie beneath freshly packed earth. There was little left of the Shallyan clerics to bury. The fire had burned with a vicious heat, consuming flesh and bone with a voracious appetite, far hotter and hungrier than any of them has seen before. Hadley has gone to search the surrounding area for any sign of Malal’s followers, leaving Tasha and Elena beside the freshly dug graves.

Tasha kneels in the mud, pale and gaunt, her soaked robes clinging to her shoulders. Her hands lie open in her lap. For some time her lips have been moving in whispered prayer, her hands gripping her pendant, the dove of Shallya, so tightly her knuckles are white. Elena stands nearby, equally pale, rain dripping from her hair and hood. She watches Tasha, uncertain whether to speak.

“I don’t know their names,” Tasha says at last. 

Elena crouches beside her. “Someone in Kethryn will know.” 

Tasha looks across the three patches of earth and her voice falters. “There might have been something inside. A book. Letters. Something to tell us who they were.” 

“We looked as carefully as we could,” Elena says. 

Tasha lowers her head, and Elena rests a hand on her shoulder. For a while they remain like that beneath the steady rain.

Just beyond the Old Shrine Road, Hadley finds tracks leading through the mud into the woodland. He follows cautiously, pausing now and then to listen. Rain ticks against the leaves overhead, masking quieter sounds, and he tightens his grip on his sword hilt.

The tracks bring him to a small clearing. Around its edges, the trees look scorched and dying, their leaves curled brown on blackened branches. One tree has been sheared almost in half. Hadley approaches it slowly. The damage looks less like a lightning strike than the work of some enormous, white-hot blade, slashing down and cutting away the branches in a single blow. Along the exposed wood, the edges are burned black.

His gaze follows a straight scorch mark down the trunk and across the ground. He steps back to judge its full extent: perhaps seven feet high and four or five feet wide. Rainwater runs through the blackened earth at his feet. Hadley looks from the scar to the surrounding trees, then turns slowly, checking the clearing again.

Burnt into the damaged trunk is a charred symbol. Hadley steps closer, trying to make it out against the charred wood. His stomach turns, vomit rising in his throat. He stops, swallows, and presses a hand against his belly until the sudden nausea eases.

The symbol of a blackened blade burnt into the wood. He recognises the mark of Malal, but there is something wrong with its shape. The edges seem to shift as he studies them, bending where they should be straight. He blinks and tries to focus. For an instant the whole symbol appears to writhe against the trunk, and bile rises in his throat.

Hadley backs away. Only when he looks elsewhere does his stomach begin to settle. He shivers beneath his wet coat, glances once more towards the tree, then leaves the clearing and heads back through the woodland towards Tasha and Elena.

Hadley sees Tasha fall. A raw scream of horror and anguish tears from him. He drives his heel into the sodden ground and surges to his feet, lifting his sword above the creature’s ruined head and bringing it down with all his strength.

Steel cleaves through blackened bone with a sickening crack, splitting the skull and driving deep into the ravaged torso. The force jars through his shoulders. For an instant he stands almost face to face with it, teeth bared, before wrenching the blade free.

The creature staggers, its split jaws sagging as the ravaged skull collapses. One claw reaches towards him, then falls away as the thing pitches into the mud at his feet. Elena drops into the mud beside him and gathers Tasha's head into her lap. A cough shakes Tasha’s body, bringing blood to her lips. Her face is terribly pale, her eyes shadowed and struggling to focus. Elena wipes at the mud on her cheek with trembling fingers.

Tasha coughs again, and blood spatters her lips. Hadley feels her chest heave beneath his hands. He holds the cloth tighter, though blood is already running pooling on the wet ground. Tasha looks up at Elena, then at Hadley. Her hand lifts and covers Hadley's, stilling it. Her lips move. Elena lowers her head to hear, still cradling her against the rain.

*The pain loosens its hold. Tasha feels herself rising, though there is no effort in it, no weight to lift. Below, she sees her own body lying in the mud. Hadley is bent over her, pressing the torn robe desperately against her chest while Elena cradles her head, tears coursing down her face. Tasha reaches towards them, but her hand passes through Elena’s shoulder. At her own feet lie the ravaged remains of the dead cleric. Looking at him now, she feels an aching sorrow, a pity so fierce she longs to kneel beside him, to comfort him somehow.*

She does not know the place, yet the sight troubles her. Then she becomes aware that someone is waiting nearby.*

*A woman stands a little way off in a simple silver-white robe. Her face is filled with sorrow, but her sad smile comforts Tasha, easing the fear she feels coursing from the torn tree. She holds out her hand.*

*"Come, child."*

*Tasha looks back at the two figures huddled over her body.*

*"Will they be alright without me?" The woman’s hand remains outstretched.*

*"Their fate is no longer twined with yours, daughter."*

*"They are my true friends. I love them."*

*Hadley bows his head, his bloodied hands trembling but still holding the cloth in place, his shoulders shaking as his grief overcomes him. Elena touches Tasha’s cheek, gently as though she fears it might hurt her. Tasha watches them, unwilling to turn away.*

*"And they have loved you," the woman says. "Come. It is time."*

Quietly, with rain falling gently on her face and her friends beside her, Tasha dies.
