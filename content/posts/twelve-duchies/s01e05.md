The three of them leave the alley at a brisk pace. Hadley leads without seeming to think about the route, choosing one busy street after another, weaving through traders hauling carts, labourers finishing the day’s work, and children darting between market stalls. “I’ve seen men like Ketter and Fen before,” Hadley says after a while. “That type travel in packs so that nobody fights back. You saw what they did to Berrin at the docks. “Shallya teaches that every life can be turned towards the light.”

Hadley smiles faintly, though there is little warmth in it.

“I hope she’s right. Truly. I hope she's right.”

“You don’t believe she is.”

“No. I’ve spent too long watching men like Ketter and Fen prove otherwise.”

For a time they walk in silence, only the noise and bustle of the city around them. Cart wheels rattle across uneven cobbles. A woman haggles over onions with theatrical outrage. Somewhere a cooper hammers iron hoops onto a barrel. Children shriek, laugh and shout nearby.

Elena walks between them in thoughtful silence. “I don’t think either of you is wrong,” she says at last. They both look towards her.

“In my homeland, we would have tried to avoid bloodshed altogether. But once steel is drawn…” She hesitates, choosing her words carefully. “Mercy can sometimes be an unwise choice. It is a hard truth but a truth nonetheless.”

Tasha frowns - “And what would your people have done?”

Elena’s gaze drifts back towards the city behind them - “We would have made sure the quarrel ended. One way or another.”

None of them speaks after that. They simply continue north, letting Kethryn swallow them amongst its countless strangers, each carrying their own thoughts towards the old shrine road.
