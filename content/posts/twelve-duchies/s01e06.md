With Kethryn's gates closed for the night, there is little to do but make camp beside the road. The growing darkness persuades even Tasha that travelling further tonight is a bad idea. Two of the merchants who stayed behind after the accident join them, bringing a small keg of ale and a few provisions. They find a sheltered patch of grass beneath the trees, far enough from the road to escape the dust haze, and soon a fire is crackling in a shallow pit.

After the strain and confusion of the afternoon, the evening is unexpectedly pleasant. Bread, hard cheese and some dried meats are passed around, along with a few apples from a nearby tree. The ale is not particularly good, but it's drinkable and Hadley doesn't complain. Around them, other travellers have made their own camps, and scattered fires glow along the roadside beneath the dark bulk of Kethryn's walls.

One of the merchants, a broad fellow with a surprisingly good singing voice, begins an old lake song about a fisherman's daughter and a sailor who makes several increasingly bad decisions. Tasha quickly picks up the chorus. Hadley is reluctant at first, then joins in after sufficient encouragement, singing badly and with a confidence lent him by the ale. Even Elena is eventually coaxed into contributing a wistful elven song, its unfamiliar melody haunting enough to quiet the conversation around the fire until she finishes.

Stories follow the songs. The merchants have tales of storms on Footprint Lake, crooked toll-keepers and wagons lost on mountain roads. Hadley tells a carefully edited story from his soldiering days that has Tasha laughing and Elena regarding him with amused suspicion. 

"It didn't happen like that," Elena says when he finishes. Hadley takes another drink. 

"You weren't there." 

"Neither were half the people in your story."

Hadley opens his mouth to protest, pauses, shrugs and then takes another pull of ale.

By the time the fire has sunk to glowing embers, the weariness of the day has caught up with them. They wrap themselves in blankets beneath a clear sky, the distant walls of Kethryn black against the stars. The road falls quiet, conversation dwindles, and sleep comes easily. For once, nothing troubles them before morning.

Dawn breaks bright and clear over Footprint Lake. The rising sun catches the water beyond Kethryn and turns it briefly to hammered gold, while the city walls throw long shadows across the road. Their little camp stirs early. Blankets are rolled, packs shouldered and the last crusts of bread eaten while the merchants coax life back into their own fire.

They take their leave not long after sunrise. Tasha is plainly impatient to be moving, having already lost the better part of a day to the accident and the closed gates. 

"Shallya's will," she says when Hadley points this out, though the tightness in her voice suggests she might have preferred Shallya to arrange things differently. Elena glances at Hadley. 

"Best not argue with the goddess." 

"Wasn't planning to," he replies, with a quick smile.

The merchants reckon the old shrine to be half a day's walk, perhaps a little more if the road is poor. Their directions are reassuringly simple: follow the North Road out of Kethryn, take the Old Shrine Road where it branches away into wooded country, and keep going.

"You'll cross an old stone bridge about halfway - that's the Pilgrim's Bridge," one tells them. "If you reach that, you're on the right road. Shrine's beyond it." Tasha listens intently, committing every detail to memory.

The North Road is already stirring as they set out, with carts creaking towards Kethryn and early travellers making the most of the cool morning. After half an hour they reach the turning. The Old Shrine Road is narrower and quieter, its worn surface leading away from the busier highway beneath the trees.

Tasha stops for a moment at the junction, gazing along it with an expression of delight. After days aboard the _Running Hind_, the distractions of Kethryn and yesterday's accident, Shallya's road finally lies beneath her feet.

"This is it," she says, almost to herself, then starts forward with renewed purpose. 

Hadley adjusts the weight of his pack and follows. Elena falls into step beside him. 

"Half a day," he mutters. "Hopefully..." 

Tasha, several paces ahead, either doesn't hear him or chooses not to.

The Old Shrine Road is markedly quieter than the North Road. Within a few minutes the creak of carts and clatter of hooves fade behind them, replaced by birdsong and the soft stir of leaves overhead. The road itself is badly overgrown, with thick tufts of grass between the old wheel ruts and brambles encroaching from either side, but as the merchants and guards at the North Gate said, it remains passable. From time to time, someone still passes this way.

For the first few hours the walking is easy but slow. The road climbs steadily into the wooded hills north-west of Kethryn, winding beneath oak and beech and occasionally offering glimpses of Footprint Lake through gaps in the trees. Tasha sets a determined pace at first, but even she slows as the forest thickens and the ground becomes steeper. 

"Definitely still Shallya's road?" Hadley asks as he shakes himself free of a persistently thorny bramble.

"Absolutely," Tasha smiles brightly. "Shallya never promises an easy road, only that you will, at some point, reach the end of it."

Under his breath Hadley mutters something mournful about the beer he left at the Hunting Heron, long since tipped into the slops bucket.

By mid-morning the character of the countryside begins to change. The forest thins, bare stone breaking through the soil and roots twisting across the path. In several places small rockfalls have spilled down the hillside, forcing them to pick their way over loose slabs and moss-covered boulders. There are still signs of other travellers - a post driven into the ground near a treacherous slope with a rope to serve as a handhold, a patch of ground trodden flat with signs of a camp fire. Elena crouches briefly beside one muddy patch. 

"Boot prints," she says, studying the faint impressions. "Not very recent though." 

Hadley nods. "Recent enough. We're not the only faithful travellers who come this way."

His voice carries a level of sarcasm which makes Tasha raise an eyebrow at him, a tolerant smile on her face, but she says nothing.

Eventually the forest thins almost to nothing, the road ending at the lip of a narrow gorge. Its sides fall steeply between walls of dark, fractured stone, tangled with ferns and trailing roots. Somewhere below, hidden by shadow and vegetation, water rushes noisily over rock.

Spanning the gorge is a narrow stone bridge, scarcely wider than a cart, its parapets worn and patched with lichen. Grass sprouts between the paving stones and small saplings have rooted themselves where the masonry meets the hillside. But despite the fact that stones have clearly recently fallen from the structure, the old arch still stands firm above the drop. Tasha stops at the sight of it, tiredness briefly forgotten. 

"Pilgrim's Bridge," she says. Elena looks across to where the road disappears once more beneath the trees. "Halfway, then." 

Hadley eyes the ancient stonework. "Looks more like an accidental pile of stones that happen to roughly resemble a bridge!"

Tasha looks from Elena to the gorge. "That makes uncomfortable sense." 

Hadley grunts, but pulls the rope free of his pack. 

"I'm a _bit_ heavier than you, not 'a lot' heavier. You'd think I was one of those tubby merchants you see stuffing themselves on feast days."

He loops it securely around Elena and checks the knot twice before winding the other end around his forearm. 

"Go slowly. Test every stone before you put your weight on it. If I tell you to stop, you stop." 

Elena nods. She is a little pale - her confidence has begun to desert her. She steps towards the bridge and peers down into the narrow gorge. Her face tightens and she immediately fixes her eyes on the far side instead. 

"I dislike this plan," she says quietly. 

Hadley plants his feet and takes up the slack in the rope. "It was your plan." 

Elena draws a careful breath. "Yes. I'm beginning to see a critical flaw in it."

Tasha gives her an encouraging smile. "Just don't look down. You'll be fine." 

Elena puts one foot onto the first weathered paving stone. "That advice would have been considerably more useful a few minutes ago."
